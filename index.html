Tokenizer Transplantation: Mitigating Autoregressive Collapse
in Edge-Efficient Bengali ASR

Sanjid Hasan1, Md. Abdur Rahman1

1Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET) · ICML 2026, MuslimML Workshop

Abstract

Moonshine-Base ships with a byte-level decoder vocabulary tuned for English, which fragments Bengali text into long token sequences and destabilizes autoregressive decoding under low-resource fine-tuning. We propose tokenizer transplantation: replacing the model's native decoder vocabulary with BanglaBERT's WordPiece vocabulary, reducing token fertility from 9.16 to 1.30 tokens per word. Direct vocabulary substitution disrupts the decoder's learned embedding space, so we recover performance through a two-stage ScheduleFree AdamW fine-tuning schedule. The resulting 61.5M-parameter model reaches 21.54% WER and 10.79% CER on the Lipi-Ghor-bn-882-SSTT test set, with a real-time factor of 0.0053 on GPU.

Tokenizer Transplantation

We replace Moonshine-Base's English byte-level decoder vocabulary with BanglaBERT's native WordPiece vocabulary, mapping the special tokens (⟨CLS⟩→BOS, ⟨SEP⟩→EOS) so the decoder's input/output space aligns with the new vocabulary. This reduces token fertility from 9.16 to 1.30 tokens per word, shortening the sequences the decoder must autoregress over.

Two-Stage Recovery Fine-Tuning

Swapping the vocabulary disrupts the decoder's learned embedding space, so we recover performance in two stages: a high-learning-rate ScheduleFree AdamW stage that re-stabilizes the decoder around the new vocabulary, followed by a reduced-learning-rate stabilization stage with dynamic masking enabled.

Results

On the Lipi-Ghor-bn-882-SSTT test set, our 61.5M-parameter model reaches 21.54% WER and 10.79% CER, with a real-time factor of 0.0053 on GPU — competitive accuracy at a fraction of the parameters and latency of larger ASR baselines.
